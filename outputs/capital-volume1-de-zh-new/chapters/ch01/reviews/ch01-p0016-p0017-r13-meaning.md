## Source argument map

- [v1-ch01-s01-p0016]: main claim: a commodity's value magnitude would remain constant if the labour time required for its production remained constant, but required labour time changes with labour productivity; steps: productivity depends on workers' average skill, the development of science and its technological applicability, the social combination of production, the scale and effectiveness of means of production, and natural conditions; equal quantities of labour therefore yield unequal quantities of wheat or metal under different natural or mining conditions; diamonds embody much labour in little volume because finding them is rare and labour-intensive; the gold and diamond price material distinguishes value from the price actually paid; richer mines or labour-saving conversion of coal into diamonds would reduce diamond value; in general, value varies directly with the quantity of labour embodied and inversely with the productivity of that labour.
- [v1-ch01-s01-p0017]: main claim: use-value, product of labour, commodity, and value are not coextensive; steps: a useful thing has no value when its usefulness is not mediated by labour; a useful product made for the producer's own need is not a commodity; production for others is necessary but not sufficient, because a commodity must pass through exchange to the person for whom it is a use-value; rent grain and tithe grain are counterexamples to the claim that every product made for others is a commodity; finally, a useless thing cannot have value because the labour contained in it is useless, does not count as labour, and forms no value.
- [note-012]: main claim: Engels inserted the bracketed qualification in the fourth edition because omitting it had frequently produced the mistaken reading that Marx counted every product consumed by someone other than its producer as a commodity.

## Claim and logic audit

- T1 [v1-ch01-s01-p0016]: the source lists “die Entwicklungsstufe der Wissenschaft und ihrer technologischen Anwendbarkeit” as one determinant of productivity: the level reached by science and the extent to which that science is technologically applicable. The draft writes “科学和技术的发展水平”, which instead coordinates the development levels of science and technology. This drops technological applicability and substitutes the development of technology as a separate object. Because this is one of the source's stated determinants, the altered list item is an unresolved omission/substitution.
- [v1-ch01-s01-p0016]: the conditional opening, the inverse movement of productivity and required labour time, the wheat and mine examples, the rarity and labour cost of finding diamonds, the possible fall in diamond value with richer mines or cheap conversion from coal, and the concluding direct/inverse relation are all preserved with the same direction and scope.
- [v1-ch01-s01-p0016]: the draft's price/value distinction correctly reconstructs the function of the Jacob and Eschwege material. “钻石的售价与价值之间，差距可能更大” is a defensible rendering of “Noch mehr gilt dies vom Diamant” in light of the immediately following comparison; the eighty-year, 1823, and one-and-a-half-year quantities and the contrast between lower price and greater labour/value are preserved.
- [v1-ch01-s01-p0016]: “钻石可以把这个关系说明得更清楚”, “这里需要区分钻石的价值和它实际卖到的价格” and “这正说明” are added organizational links. Each is supported by the source's sequence: the diamond material instantiates the labour/value relation, explicitly contrasts price with represented labour/value, and then returns to the effect of labour requirements on diamond value. They add no independent doctrine.
- T2 [v1-ch01-s01-p0017]: “wenn sein Nutzen für den Menschen nicht durch Arbeit vermittelt ist” states that the thing's usefulness for humans is not mediated through labour. The draft narrows this to “如果它的用处不是由劳动创造的”. “Not created by labour” is not equivalent to “not mediated by labour”: labour may mediate a naturally given usefulness without creating that usefulness. The Chinese therefore supplies a different criterion for when a use-value lacks value and risks classifying a labour-mediated natural useful thing by the wrong test. This conceptual change is unresolved.
- [v1-ch01-s01-p0017]: the remaining distinctions are preserved: self-use production creates use-value but not a commodity; usefulness to others alone is insufficient; rent grain and tithe grain do not become commodities merely because they are produced for others; transfer through exchange is required; and a useless object cannot form value because its contained labour is useless. “不能算作形成价值的劳动” makes explicit the local value-forming scope of “zählt nicht als Arbeit” without changing the conclusion.
- [v1-ch01-s01-p0017]: the square-bracketed Chinese passage covers the same qualification as the source's bracketed passage. “封建主” and “教会” preserve the relevant recipients and the rent/tithe contrast at the level needed for the argument, although “Pfaffen” is more literally a priest or clergy member.
- [note-012]: the fourth-edition status, Engels's act of insertion, the frequent misunderstanding, and the mistaken criterion “由生产者以外的人消费” are all preserved. The attribution to Engels is also retained.
