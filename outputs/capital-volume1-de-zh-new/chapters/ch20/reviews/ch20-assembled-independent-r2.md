## Overall assessment

An ordinary undergraduate can recover the section's main claim after one reading: international wage comparison cannot stop at nominal daily or weekly pay, because countries differ in subsistence costs, working time, intensity, productivity, and output per worker. A more developed country may therefore have higher nominal wages while imposing a lower labour cost per unit of output. The theoretical mechanism is followed by factory evidence, a qualification about comparability, a practical railway example, and a critique of an attempted universal wage-productivity law.

The text keeps nominal wage, purchasing power, relative labour price, and employer cost sufficiently distinct to support the argument. Two non-blocking S risks are noted below. There is no unresolved T defect, so the verdict is PASS.

## Paragraph findings

- C1: The opening states why wage movements can diverge from purchasing power and gives a concrete checklist for cross-country comparison. The final conversion from daily wage to equal-length working days and then to piece wages follows from the stated need to include productivity and intensity. The paragraph is long, but its “因此” conclusion clearly closes the list. No T finding.
- C2-C4: The text first constructs national and world averages of labour intensity, then adds productivity, then draws the nominal-wage/real-wage distinction. The conditional clause limiting when higher productivity is treated as higher intensity is grammatically attached to the correct claim. The sentence “这完全不等于……” prevents the reader from confusing higher nominal wages with more means of subsistence. No T finding.
- C5-C6: The first note reinforces the difference between cheaper goods and a rise in labour-power's price; the second openly defers exceptions in individual branches. The deferred issue is marked rather than falsely settled. No T finding.
- C7: The apparent paradox is stated directly: the developed country can have higher time wages while the less developed country has the higher relative labour price. “后者” has only one viable antecedent, the less developed country, and the final sentence defines the comparison ratio. No T finding.
- C8 and the two tables: The evidence moves from an English/continental comparison to German and Russian examples, then to factory size and spindles per worker. The two table headings and units make clear what is being compared. Despite the paragraph's length, every example serves the same proposition: low wages can coexist with higher labour cost per product because output per worker is lower. No T finding.
- C9: Redgrave's qualification explains why the raw factory comparison is biased against England, then supplies a more nearly like-for-like mill example. The numbers, workers, spindles, daily output, and yarn length have identifiable roles. No T finding.
- C10: Anderson's note distinguishes the worker's apparent daily pay from the employer's cost of obtaining a given quantity of work, and the Ireland quotation restates the low-wage/high-cost result. S: “劳动的实际价格” in this historical quotation sits near the main text's “实际工资” and “相对劳动价格”, so a reader may briefly compare unlike measures; the note immediately defines the former as the employer's cost for a quantity of work, preventing a material confusion.
- C11: The railway example converts the preceding statistics into a practical operating result and then states the chapter's thesis in plain language: higher wages often coincide with lower labour cost per unit of product. No T finding.
- C12: The paragraph first states Carey's proposed productivity-wage rule, then says the statistical premise is unproved, and finally traces his successive appeals to state intervention, protectionism, theory, and trade. S: the extended ironic critique carries several successive targets in one paragraph and is heavier than the rest of the chapter, but repeated references to Carey and explicit transitions keep the object of each criticism recoverable. No T finding.
- C13: The final note is a bibliographic identification of Carey's work and creates no comprehension risk. No T finding.

## Failure probes

- Main line: The section moves from the variables needed for international comparison to world-market intensity/productivity, then to the nominal-wage versus labour-cost result, evidence, operational confirmation, and a rejected overgeneralization. The evidence returns to the announced comparison rather than forming an unrelated list. Result: no T.
- First-use terminology: “名义工资” is immediately contrasted with what workers can obtain; “相对劳动价格” is defined as a ratio to surplus value and product value; the historical phrase “实际劳动价格” is separately defined as employer cost for a quantity of work. The distinctions remain recoverable despite the S terminology risk. Result: no T.
- Referents: “这种生产率更高的劳动”, “这些产量”, “后者”, “这项比较”, “这些数字”, “它们”, and the repeated “他” in the Carey paragraph have clear local antecedents. Result: no T.
- Connectors and actual relations: “另一方面”, “因此”, “只要”, “由此”, “不过”, “即使”, “何况”, “按照他的解释”, “后来”, and “最后” correctly signal addition, condition, inference, qualification, concession, and sequence. None substitutes for a missing relation essential to the wage comparison. Result: no T.
- Scope and comparisons: Equal-length working days are required before daily wages are compared; piece wages are introduced to reflect intensity and productivity; developed and less developed countries are compared separately by nominal wage, worker consumption, and employer cost; the factory caveat narrows the statistical comparison before an example is offered. Result: no T.
- Paragraph and table structure: C1, C8, C10, and C12 are long, but each has a stable question and visible endpoint. The two tables have distinct measures and do not invite cross-column confusion. Footnotes supplement definitions or evidence and do not contain a missing bridge needed by the main prose. Result: no T.
